01 / FINANCIAL STATEMENT ANALYSIS

Growth, profitability, and cash generation

Revenue growth = Revenueₜ / Revenueₜ₋₁ − 1 Operating margin = Operating income / Revenue FCF proxy = Cash from operations − PP&E purchases

Annual company-facts records are selected by issuer, fiscal period, filing metadata and metric units. Calculated measures are displayed only when the required reported inputs exist.

Checks: fiscal-year ordering, source lineage, negative cash flow, null values and mismatched filing periods.

02 / PORTFOLIO STATISTICS

Covariance and risk estimation

rₜ = Pₜ / Pₜ₋₁ − 1 Portfolio variance = wᵀΣw Annual volatility = sd(r) × √sessions/year

Price observations are aligned before return calculations. The optimizer applies covariance stabilization and evaluates portfolio variance under feasible investment-weight limits.

Checks: price validity, matching dates, covariance symmetry, positive variance and valid annualization conventions.

Optimizer source · Tests

03 / NUMERICAL OPTIMIZATION

Constrained allocation

Minimize wᵀΣw subject to Σwᵢ = 1 and 0 ≤ wᵢ ≤ cap

Optimized portfolios are compared against an equal-weight baseline. Cap constraints and objective feasibility are validated before reporting results.

Checks: sum-to-one, cap compliance, infeasible constraints, objective-value reconciliation and comparison against the fitted baseline.

Implementation · Tests

04 / OUT-OF-SAMPLE TIME SERIES

Walk-forward strategy evaluation

Fit on times 1…T → evaluate on T+1…T+h Net wealth = gross wealth − modeled trading costs

The evaluation separates past-only training windows from later test periods, compares allocation rules on matched windows, and tracks fees, drawdowns and sensitivity to modeling choices.

Checks: no future-data leakage, nonoverlapping test periods, fee reconciliation, passive baseline and leave-one-window-out sensitivity.

Robustness implementation · Tests

05 / DISCOUNTED CASH FLOW

Scenario sensitivity and terminal-value exposure

FCFₜ = Revenue₀ × (1 + g)ᵗ × assumed margin PV = Σ [FCFₜ/(1+r)ᵗ] + TV/(1+r)ᴺ

SEC financials define the observed baseline; growth, margin, terminal assumptions and discounting are explicit scenario inputs. Terminal-value share measures sensitivity of the modeled proxy.

Checks: discount-rate bounds, missing cash-flow inputs, terminal share and changing growth/discount assumptions. This proxy is not a verified stock-price target.

Provenance, regression testing, and failure cases

Evidence = {source, date range, settings, model version, results, dataset fingerprint, checks}

Historical observation windows, configurable assumptions, source provenance and SHA-256 fingerprints support reproducible comparisons. Automated Python and JavaScript regression tests exercise expected results and reject invalid inputs.

QUANTITATIVE FINANCE / NUMERICAL OPTIMIZATION

01. Constrained portfolio optimization

Allocation weights are fitted from observed returns and an estimated covariance matrix, subject to feasibility and concentration limits.

Implemented privately
min wᵀΣw   subject to   Σwᵢ = 1;   0 ≤ wᵢ ≤ cap

Why it matters: the objective is predicted portfolio variance, not a guarantee of realized return. The private optimizer supports a covariance-based minimum-risk allocation and alternative inverse-volatility / return-tilt allocations.

  • Align price histories to comparable observations before estimating returns.
  • Apply covariance stabilization/shrinkage and reject impossible weight caps.
  • Compare annualized volatility, drawdown, tail loss and alternative allocations.
Implementation and verification detail

Estimated portfolio volatility is √(wᵀΣw) × √annualization; optimization remains sensitive to return sampling, correlated assets and constraints. Tests check symmetry, finite prices, positive covariance diagonals, feasibility and weight normalization.

STATISTICAL VALIDATION / TIME SERIES

02. Rolling walk-forward evaluation

A model trains on past observations, then is evaluated on the following unseen sessions. The allocation is refitted at later boundaries.

Implemented privately
Train[t₀:t₁] → Fit w₁ → Test[t₁:t₂] → Roll forward

The private study supports non-overlapping evaluation windows, modeled turnover and basis-point transaction fees, two rebalanced strategies, and a passive equal-weight buy-and-hold comparison.

  • Run each allocation rule against matched evaluation dates.
  • Reconcile per-window fees to total reported costs.
  • Perform leave-one-window-out diagnostics; a changed winner signals sample dependence.
  • Preserve observations and model settings in versioned research manifests.
Where bias can still enter

Past-only fitting reduces mechanical look-ahead within the test, but cannot eliminate survivorship bias, post-hoc asset selection or tuning parameters after seeing outcomes. Omitting a window without refitting is a sensitivity check, not independent validation.

FINANCIAL MODELING / SEC DISCLOSURES

03. SEC-backed forecast sensitivity

Observed company financials are kept distinct from hypothetical revenue-growth, cash-flow-margin and discounting assumptions.

Real source + modeled scenarios
FCFₜ = Revenue₀(1+g)ᵗ × margin
PV = Σ FCFₜ/(1+r)ᵗ + TV/(1+r)ᴺ

Real SEC annual statements establish the observed baseline. The model projects three- to seven-year hypothetical scenarios and produces a sensitivity grid for revenue growth and the discount rate.

  • Report free cash-flow proxy as operating cash flow less cash PP&E purchases.
  • Distinguish discounted cash-flow proxy from an equity value or share-price target.
  • Display how much present value comes from the terminal component.
  • Reject impossible assumptions and missing SEC cash flows instead of fabricating data.
Terminal-value risk

Terminal value extrapolates cash flows beyond the explicit projection horizon. When the terminal share dominates, small changes in discount and perpetual growth rates can materially change the result. Debt, excess cash and capital-structure adjustments are not included in this proxy.

How scenarios respond to assumptions

DATA SCIENCE / EXPLAINABLE RESEARCH

04. Historical investment screening

A private shortlist compares price momentum, realized volatility and drawdown, while displaying available SEC fundamentals separately.

Owner-only research
Score = Σ (lens weightₖ × normalized metricₖ)

Growth, balanced and risk-aware lenses change how much weight the model places on trailing return, recent momentum, annualized volatility and historical drawdown. The score is an explainable heuristic, not a probability of gain.

  • Use selectable investment names rather than requiring ticker memorization.
  • Flag sparse histories and report the period and IEX/SIP source.
  • Show SEC revenue growth and FCF margins only when disclosed facts support them.
  • Keep ranking and trade execution separate; no brokerage orders are placed.
Why a top-ranked investment may still be unattractive

Momentum and low observed volatility do not measure whether a stock is expensive relative to its future cash flows. Future returns also depend on valuation, concentration, news and unforeseeable events. The research lens is for comparing candidates, not picking guaranteed winners.

Finance terms · plain-English reference

Volatility: how much prices moved up and down in the observed period.

Drawdown: how far a portfolio fell from a prior high.

Backtest: a calculation using past prices, not proof of future success.

Walk-forward: a backtest that repeatedly fits on earlier data and checks later observations.

Cash flow: money reported moving through a company; this is not the same as stock-price return.

Concentration: how dependent your portfolio value is on one holding.

SEC filing: a company disclosure filed with the US securities regulator.

FCF proxy: operating cash flow minus cash spent on property and equipment; not a stock-price target.

Discount rate: a model assumption about the value of future cash flow in today’s dollars.

Correlation: how two observed return series moved together; historical correlation can change.

Before acting: a research checklist
  1. Can I explain what the company or fund owns and how it makes money?
  2. Are my financial figures reported, current, and comparable across periods?
  3. What do the historical risk and walk-forward results disagree about?
  4. How would this change concentration or cash in my portfolio?
  5. What information is still missing, and would a possible loss affect money I need soon?

Use the analysis links in your generated report to answer each question. This is a review tool, not a buy/sell signal.
